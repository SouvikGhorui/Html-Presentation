TARGETED CANCER THERAPY — KRAS G12C INHIBITORS

Protein–Ligand Interactions & Drug Mechanism

Covalent inhibitors selectively block mutant KRAS — precision oncology breakthrough

MRTX849 (Adagrasib)

FDA Approved Dec 2022

Covalently binds mutant Cys-12 residue (PDB: 6USZ)

Occupies Switch-II pocket (S-IIP) exclusively in G12C

Locks KRAS in GDP-bound inactive state

Disrupts KRAS–effector protein interactions

First-in-class for advanced NSCLC treatment

AMG-510 (Sotorasib)

FDA Approved May 2021

Irreversible covalent binding at Cys-12 site

Exploits unique Switch-II binding pocket

Blocks MAPK/ERK & PI3K/AKT downstream pathways

First KRAS inhibitor approved for clinical use

10-year journey from 'undruggable' to approved drug

SIGNIFICANCE

First exploitation of the KRAS G12C pocket — proving that 'undruggable' oncogenes can be targeted through precision bioinformatics-guided drug design.
